FreeFlow platform layers

Identity, accounts and the kernel serve at the bottom. Owned and shared objects sit on them, and the editors sit on top. Each layer serves the one above it and knows nothing about it.

1 · Identity

Who is signing in

A principal is a person or an agent and nothing more. A person signs in by password or Google. An agent signs in only by token and always has a person as sponsor. An MCP grant is a third way in: it acts as one of a person's accounts, limited by scopes.

2 · Tenancy

What the organization gives a member

Any person can create an organization and owns it. Each member, person or agent, gets one account: an address at the organization's domain, a hosted mailbox, calendars, a role. The organization keeps the account when the person leaves.

3 · Kernel

Services every app uses

The Worker resolves the caller once per request. The event bus is an append-only log per organization, apart from the sockets: every app publishes into it, and a subscription with a cursor reads what it missed at any time. The share index answers "shared with me". One tool registry serves /mcp, the bridge and the assistant. The shell reads app manifests.

4 · Objects

Owned, then shared

Every object has an owner and sits in a container. A grant names an account, a channel, the organization or a link, with one of four roles. Access inherits down the container chain. Grants are evaluated inside the app's own object, with no call out.

5 · Apps

The editors

Chat and Docs are one object per organization. Mail and Calendar are one object per account: Mail speaks IMAP and SMTP to a mailbox at the provider, Calendar is its own store and does its own scheduling. Each app is declared by a manifest. Mini apps and the agent bridge are apps too.
